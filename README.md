# reverie

_What happens when a digital screen stops asking for your attention and simply behaves like paper?_

reverie is an off-duty visual study exploring how a digital display can behave like a physical art print rather than an interactive interface. Modern web design often relies on high-contrast hooks, constant motion, and utility-driven triggers that demand clicks and immediate engagement. This piece intentionally steps back from those conventions to create a quiet, unhurried pocket of stillness on the screen. The layout invites the viewer to simply pause and look rather than scan for tasks, prioritizing stillness over utility. A cool off-white canvas paired with muted slate-blue serif typography softens screen glare and mimics natural ink resting on textured cotton paper. Three physical subjects—plush fabric, brushed metal, and frosted glass—are framed with generous negative space and subtle corner roundness to echo gallery prints. Subtle hover states—a slow gallery zoom and a hairline colophon underline—are whisper-quiet micro-interactions used to preserve the calm atmosphere without breaking the illusion of stillness.
